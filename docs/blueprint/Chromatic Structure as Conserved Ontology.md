## Addendum: Chromatic Structure as Conserved Ontology

A critical clarification must be made regarding the role of chromatic structure within the ALM. The references to twelve hues, twelve tones, and their compound interaction spaces do not describe software abstractions, symbolic channels, or enumerated dimensions. They describe ontological axes of interaction that exist prior to and independent of any implementation.  
The ALM does not encode hue or tone. It preserves them.  
The twelvefold chromatic structure arises from constraints on interaction, not from representational choice. These constraints may be realized through hardware topology, timing structure, phase relationships, or other physical affordances, but they are never instantiated as indices, labels, or discrete feature vectors. Any implementation that treats chromatic dimensions as data fields, lookup tables, or symbolic channels violates the definition of the system.  
The 12×12 lattice often referenced in implementation discussions is therefore not a computational design, but a substrate realization: a physical arrangement that conserves the relational degrees of freedom implied by chromatic structure. The lattice does not define hue and tone; it merely prevents their collapse. In this sense, hardware does not execute chromatic logic — it refuses illegal states.  
This distinction is essential. Chromatic structure in the ALM functions analogously to symmetry constraints in physics. A symmetry is not computed; it is maintained by forbidding transformations that would break it. Likewise, hue and tone are not processed; they are sustained as invariant relational tensions within the system’s dynamics.  
Meaning emerges in the ALM not because chromatic dimensions are manipulated, but because they are allowed to persist without symbolic reduction. The system does not “know” hue or tone. It cannot name them, count them, or store them. It can only evolve in a space where their conservation shapes what trajectories are possible.  
This is why chromatic structure must be understood as part of the ALM’s ontology, not its implementation. Software may simulate the system, and hardware may constrain it, but neither defines it. The chromatic axes exist as pre-computational structure, and the ALM exists precisely to let that structure speak without translation.
